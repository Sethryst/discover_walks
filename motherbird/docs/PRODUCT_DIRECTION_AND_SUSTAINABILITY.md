# Walk & Wildlife: Product Direction and Sustainability

## Product promise

Walk & Wildlife is a private, walking-first field journal. It helps people form a relationship with nearby nature, history, and their own experience of a walk. It is not a step leaderboard, an ad-supported map, or a system for extracting location data.

The product test is simple: does a feature deepen attention, agency, or connection? If it does not, it is probably noise.

## The experience to build

### Discovery loops, not generic routes

Route planning should begin with an intention such as **move gently**, **look for water**, **learn something local**, or **take the long way**. It should produce several genuine round-trip options, named for what they offer:

- *Elizabeth River Wildlife Loop*
- *Old Mill and Creek Walk*
- *Quiet Garden Circuit*

Time and distance are supporting facts. A route should introduce one or two compelling discoveries, not a long POI itinerary.

### A small number of meaningful prompts

During a walk, surface at most one or two relevant discoveries. Rank them by the user’s interests, the route, distance, previous visits, and local importance. A quiet walk with no prompt is a successful outcome, too.

### Place cards, not raw map data

OpenStreetMap and public datasets are the geographic foundation. Walk & Wildlife turns selected places into an experience layer:

- a short, sourced story;
- one inviting question rather than a trivia dump;
- an optional seasonal nature note;
- a clearly labelled source and editorial status.

Large parks, trails, and historic districts should be containers with filter-aware sections rather than hundreds of pins.

### The user’s own voice

Observations and reflections use the user’s language first. Taxonomy, species suggestions, and tags are optional aids—not requirements. Keep private by default.

After a walk, offer exactly one optional tell-back prompt, rotated gently:

- What did you notice that surprised you?
- Describe one place you will remember.
- What changed between the beginning and end of this walk?

Future additions may include local audio reflections and a simple sketch pad. Neither should be required to complete a walk.

### Gentle habits

Celebrate consistency and return, not volume or competition. Useful signals include days noticed, reflections kept, and observations made. Avoid leaderboards, pressure language, and species-counting as a status contest.

## What not to become

- An ad-supported location-data product.
- A dense map of uncurated OSM objects.
- A gamified collection loop that pressures people to keep moving.
- A social-performance feed.
- A health app that makes medical or fitness claims.

## Phased roadmap

### 1. The coherent core

1. Curated discovery loops with 3–5 ranked, genuine round-trip options.
2. A dedicated planning mode with no POI interruption.
3. A limited, relevance-ranked discovery layer (one or two prompts per walk).
4. Tell-back reflection after a walk.
5. Personal observation naming, optional tags, and a private-only default.

### 2. A richer local field journal

1. Curated place cards and container pages for large parks, trails, and districts.
2. Local, seasonal species guides tied to a place.
3. Audio reflection and sketch prompts.
4. Connections between nearby natural and historical stories, presented as invitations rather than explanations.

### 3. Sustainable local expansion

1. A repeatable editorial workflow for new regions.
2. Partnerships with libraries, historic societies, parks, and conservancies.
3. Downloadable regional editions with maps, routes, and curated stories.

## Funding model

### Free: the personal journal

The free app should always include the essential private experience:

- locally stored walks, observations, and reflections;
- basic map and nearby discovery;
- manual location and ordinary route planning where available;
- export and deletion of personal data;
- no advertising and no sale of personal location data.

Personal history must never be held hostage by a subscription.

### Paid: Field Editions

Offline access is a good paid-edition candidate **when it is packaged as durable regional value**, not as a punishment for being offline. A Field Edition can include:

- downloadable, offline maps and walking route data for a region;
- curated route collections and place stories;
- seasonal field guides and audio walks;
- dependable offline search and navigation;
- print-ready or expanded journal exports.

Possible pricing should be tested with users, but a one-time regional purchase (for example, $4–10) plus an optional annual all-regions membership is more aligned with local exploration than forcing a high monthly subscription.

Core privacy tools, existing journals, and access to already purchased regional downloads remain available without an active membership.

### Partner-funded editions

Libraries, historical societies, parks, conservancies, and tourism organisations can underwrite a region or collection. Partners fund editorial work and regional access—not user surveillance. Any sponsored collection is plainly labelled and held to the same editorial/source standard as every other place card.

### Optional patronage

Offer a transparent “fund the next local guide” contribution for people who want to support expansion. State what the contribution pays for: source research, editorial review, accessibility, and map packaging.

## Principles for every paid feature

1. Charge for durable value: editorial work, regional packaging, and dependable offline capability.
2. Never charge for privacy, deletion, or access to a person’s own journal.
3. Never make money from individual routes, observations, or location history.
4. Keep sponsorship visible and editorially separate.
5. Make the free edition feel whole, not like a prolonged trial.

## Product metrics that fit the philosophy

Use aggregate, opt-in product metrics only where needed to improve the app. Favor:

- completed walks with an optional reflection;
- return visits to a place or route;
- use of a downloaded regional edition;
- user-rated relevance of a discovery;
- partner renewal and funded regional access.

Do not optimize for screen time, prompt clicks, POI count, location precision, or social comparison.
